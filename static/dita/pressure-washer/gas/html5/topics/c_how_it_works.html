How the pressure washer works

The PW-3400G takes water from a standard garden hose and uses a pump to raise its pressure to as much as 3,400 psi (234 bar). The spray gun and nozzle turn that pressure into a focused stream that removes dirt, mildew and loose paint.

The path water takes

Water enters the pump through the garden hose inlet. A screen in the inlet catches debris that could damage the pump. The gasoline engine drives the pump, which pushes the water through the high-pressure hose to the spray gun. The nozzle on the end of the spray wand narrows the stream and sets the spray pattern.

What happens when you release the trigger

When you release the trigger, water has nowhere to go. An unloader valve senses the rise in pressure and sends the water back through the pump in a loop. This is called bypass mode. On the gas model, the engine keeps running in bypass mode.

Water that loops in bypass mode heats up. A thermal relief valve releases hot water from the pump before it can damage the seals. If you see water dripping from under the pump after a long pause, the valve is working as designed.

Main components

Garden hose inlet
Connects the water supply. Contains the inlet screen.
Pump
Triplex plunger pump with serviceable oil. Built for daily commercial use.
Engine
212 cc overhead-valve engine with recoil start and low-oil shutdown.
High-pressure hose
Carries pressurized water from the pump to the spray gun.
Spray gun and wand
The trigger starts and stops the spray. The trigger lock prevents accidental spraying.
Quick-connect nozzles
Color-coded tips that set the spray angle.

Choosing a nozzle

A narrower spray angle delivers more force to a smaller area. Start with the widest nozzle that does the job, and move to a narrower one only if needed.

Color Angle Typical use
Red 0° Small, stubborn spots on concrete or metal. Never use on wood, siding or vehicles.
Yellow 15° Stripping loose paint and cleaning concrete.
Green 25° General cleaning of decks, driveways and patio furniture.
White 40° Rinsing vehicles, windows and siding.
Black 65° Applying detergent at low pressure.
