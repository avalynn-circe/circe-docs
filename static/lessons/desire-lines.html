An interactive lesson · Stated values and revealed choices

Desire Lines

Name the ideal you want to live by. Then make 25 ordinary choices that never mention it. At the end, see the path your ideal would have paved, next to the trail your choices actually wore into the grass.

Instructional design by Avalynn Circe. Structured with the PARTS Method: Problem, Answer, Reasoning, Terms, Syntax.

A reflection exercise, not a psychological test. Ideas are paraphrased from these sources:

  • Leon Festinger, A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance (1957).
  • Paul A. Samuelson, "A Note on the Pure Theory of Consumer's Behaviour," Economica (1938).
  • James Blake, "Overcoming the 'Value-Action Gap' in Environmental Policy," Local Environment (1999).
  • Chris Argyris and Donald Schön, Theory in Practice: Increasing Professional Effectiveness (1974).
  • The term "desire line" comes from landscape architecture and urban planning.
