The Juniper Falls Charter

A new town is writing its charter, one clause per shared thing: bread, a pool, a meadow, a creek, a forest. Test each one, stamp it with a way of governing, and run ten years to see which stamps hold.

Charter day

The founders of Juniper Falls have read one famous essay, and it gave them two options for anything shared: sell it to an owner, or let the state run it. Stamp each of the four clauses below, then run the town for ten years.

How the charter works

Each card is something the town shares. Each stamp is a way of governing it: a market of owners and buyers, a club with dues, the state with taxes and officials, a community of users making their own rules, or layered arrangements that link local groups inside a larger framework. Running ten years scores how well each card does, from 0 to 100, based on patterns Ostrom and others reported in real cases. The scores are a simplified teaching model, not measurements.
