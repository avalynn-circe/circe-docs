Style system

Ovenbird voice and tone

A one-page guide for the fictional brand. Every rewrite above follows it.

A calm expert beside you, not across the desk.

Lead with what it means for you

Start with what happened and how it affects the reader. Terms of art come second and get defined once.

SayYour fund’s fee drops to 0.35% on May 1.
NotEffective 05/01, the Board has approved a revision to the expense ratio.

Calm, not cheerful

Money news is rarely exciting and rarely an emergency. We report, explain and offer a next step.

SayYour balance is down 8% this week.
NotALERT: Market Volatility

Specific beats soothing

A fact reassures more than a feeling does. Tell people what is true, and they can decide how to feel.

SayYour money is still invested. Nothing has been sold.
NotDon’t worry! Markets go up and down.

Never a dead end

Every message ends with something to do: a button, a phone number, a date.

SayTry again, or use another account.
NotPlease contact your financial institution.

The tone dial: same voice, different volume

Routine

Confirmations, statements, settings. Brief and warm.

Your $500 is on its way. It should arrive by March 5.

Watchful

Market moves, fee changes, tax season. Steady, with context.

Your balance is down 8% this week. Here’s what that means for your plan.

Urgent

Failed payments, deadlines, security. Direct. The first sentence says what to do.

Set up your RMD by December 31 to avoid an IRS penalty.

Mechanics

  • Sentence case for headings and buttons.
  • Buttons start with a verb and say what happens: Import clients, not Submit.
  • No exclamation points in money messages.
  • Round to whole dollars in summaries. Show cents on statements and receipts.
  • Write “down 8%,” not “−8%,” so it reads correctly aloud.
  • Link text names its destination.
  • Push notifications: title under 40 characters, body under 110.
  • Email: subject under 50 characters, preview text under 90.

Draft 1 · Rollovers landing page

Unlock Your Financial Future with Seamless Rollovers!13

Leverage Ovenbird’s best-in-class platform to consolidate your retirement assets and optimize your journey toward financial freedom.2

5

alt="Happy retired couple on beach"

Learn More4

  1. 1Should fix

    On the headline

    This headline could sit on any bank’s homepage. Someone landing here wants to know whether moving an old 401(k) is a hassle. Can the headline answer that? For example: “Move an old 401(k) without chasing paperwork.”

  2. 2Must fix

    On “seamless” and “best-in-class”

    Readers can’t check these claims, and Compliance will likely ask us to prove “best-in-class.” What can we say that’s true and specific? “We contact your old plan for you” does more work than “seamless.”

  3. 3Consider

    On the exclamation point

    Our voice guide skips exclamation points in money messages. The calm version reads as more confident, not less excited.

  4. 4Must fix

    On the button

    “Learn more” doesn’t say what happens next. If it starts the rollover, try “Start my rollover.” If it opens an explainer, try “See how rollovers work.” We could test both.

  5. 5Should fix

    On the image’s alt text

    This describes the photo but not why it’s there. If the image is decorative, use empty alt text. If it carries meaning, say what we want readers to take from it.

What I hold to in every review

  • Start from the reader’s question.Every note ties back to what the person on the page is trying to find out.
  • Name the problem, not the person.“This headline could sit anywhere,” never “you made it generic.”
  • Pair every note with a suggestion.And label it Must fix, Should fix or Consider, so the writer knows where to spend time.
  • Bring Legal and Compliance in early.Claims like “best-in-class” get flagged in the draft, not the week before launch.
